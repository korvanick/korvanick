Family Stories

At rest · Jun 2025 → Nov 2025 · Built with Gramps

I've always known half my ancestors were from Finland. What I never knew was how far back it went, or how detailed a history there was. I've been learning a lot about my connections to Slovakia and Croatia on my mother's side as well. There are many documents available, and several people on both sides of the family are deeply interested in their own research, which gives me a good base to work from and build on. The work here is taking the time to piece it together into something that paints a clearer picture of who I am.

On my Earth Expedition I learned quite a bit on the Finnish side. The history of my great-grandfather, ideas about how he and my great-grandmother met in Finland, the year he emigrated, and more. Visa constraints and travel fatigue kept me from connecting with and interviewing family while I was in Zagreb, and I have no known connections in Slovakia yet.

I've installed Gramps locally and started entering information. The long-term goal is to have it running on my server with off-site backups, several family members able to contribute, and the ability to export the data as updates are made so everyone else has a copy. I know it is feasible, it is free and open source, and it seems like the best option.

How far back it goes

The earliest known relatives on each side vary drastically. On the Finnish side, records go back seven generations in great detail, with some going as far as the 1400s. There is extensive research going on into the lineage that may identify which part of the country those family members were really from.

On my mother's side the dates go back to the 1800s, and this is where the information requires deeper understanding. There is a lot of depth there, and a lot of stories written down, even though it is only four generations back. Some documents say Austria, others say Croatia. A complicated regional history muddies the waters of where anyone was actually from. Two great-great-grandparents were certainly from modern-day Slovakia, at the time part of Austria-Hungary. The rest likely all came from what is now Croatia, though not all are confirmed yet.
